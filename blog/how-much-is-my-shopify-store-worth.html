How Much Is My Shopify Store Worth? The 2026 Valuation Guide

Valuation · July 10, 2026 · 7 min read

Short answer: most Shopify stores sell for a multiple of their annual net profit — typically between 1.5× and 4×, depending on how transferable and durable that profit is. A dropshipping store clearing $50,000 a year in verified net profit will usually attract offers somewhere between $75,000 and $200,000. The entire game of exiting well is moving yourself toward the top of that range.

This guide breaks down how buyers actually calculate the number, what pushes your multiple up or down, and why a live, data-connected valuation beats any generic online calculator.

The formula buyers actually use

Nearly every e-commerce acquisition is priced from one number: SDE — Seller's Discretionary Earnings. It's your net profit plus anything you pay yourself (salary, personal expenses run through the business) added back in.

Store value = SDE × multiple

The multiple is where negotiation lives. It reflects one question in the buyer's head: "How confident am I that this profit continues after the founder walks away?" The more confident they are, the more years of profit they'll pay for upfront.

What moves your multiple up

  • Clean, verifiable financials. A tidy P&L that matches your Shopify and payment processor data is the single fastest trust-builder. Deals fall apart when numbers don't reconcile.
  • Healthy net margin. Buyers benchmark against your niche. A 20%+ net margin on a dropshipping store signals pricing power and disciplined ad spend.
  • Repeat customers and email revenue. Revenue that doesn't depend on cold ads is worth more per dollar. A 15–25% repeat rate or a list that drives 10%+ of sales meaningfully raises multiples.
  • Low refund and chargeback rates. High refunds scream product risk. Under ~3% keeps buyers comfortable.
  • Documented operations. SOPs for fulfillment, customer service, and ad management make the store feel like a machine, not a founder-dependent hustle.
  • Diversified traffic. One winning ad account is a single point of failure. Organic, email, and multi-channel paid traffic all de-risk the deal.

What drags it down

  • Declining trend lines. A store falling 20% quarter over quarter gets priced on where it's going, not where it's been.
  • Single-product concentration. If one SKU is 90% of revenue, the buyer is buying a product lifecycle, not a business.
  • Ad fatigue. Rising CPAs on your core creatives suggest the easy growth is behind you — buyers model that in.
  • Supplier fragility. No backup supplier, no negotiated terms, long shipping times — all discount the price.
  • Messy books. If a buyer has to reconstruct your finances, they compensate themselves for that work with a lower offer.
