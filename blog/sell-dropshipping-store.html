How to Sell a Dropshipping Store: The Complete Exit Playbook

Exits · July 10, 2026 · 8 min read

Selling a dropshipping store isn't like selling a domain or flipping a product. You're transferring a living system — traffic, suppliers, data, and cash flow — to someone who needs to trust all of it before wiring real money. Done right, it's a 30–60 day process. Done wrong, it's six months of tire-kickers and a lowball exit, or worse, a leaked store idea and no deal at all.

Here's the full playbook, in the order deals actually happen.

Step 1: Know your number before anyone else does

Everything starts with a defensible valuation. Buyers price dropshipping stores on SDE multiples (typically 1.5–4× annual net profit — full breakdown here). If you walk into conversations without knowing your range, you'll anchor low or scare buyers off high.

Get your valuation before deciding to sell. It also tells you whether waiting 60–90 days and fixing two or three metrics would add five figures to your exit.

Step 2: Build your data room

Serious buyers ask for the same package every time. Prepare it once, cleanly:

  • P&L statement — last 12 months minimum, reconciled against Shopify payouts and ad spend.
  • Traffic and ad account evidence — screenshots or shared reports showing spend, ROAS, and creative history.
  • Supplier details — agreements, shipping times, backup options, and whether relationships transfer.
  • SOPs — how orders flow, how support is handled, how ads are managed. Even simple one-pagers raise buyer confidence.
  • Customer data summary — email list size, repeat rate, refund rate, chargeback history.
Shortcut: the Dexits app builds this automatically — an automated exit checklist that organizes, verifies, and preps every asset a buyer expects, straight from your store data.

Step 3: Decide how you'll find buyers

You have three routes:

  • Public marketplaces. Maximum eyeballs, minimum privacy. Your competitors, suppliers, and customers can all see your numbers — and copycats can see your winning products. Listing fees and success fees usually apply.
  • Brokers. White-glove but expensive (often 10–15% commission), and most won't touch smaller dropshipping deals.
  • Private buyer networks. Your store is shared as an anonymized profile with pre-verified buyers only. No public listing, no info leaks. This is the model Dexits runs — buyers must submit an LOI and proof of funds before they see your store's identity.

Step 4: Screen hard — LOI and proof of funds first

The single biggest time-waster in store sales is the unqualified buyer. Protect yourself with two gates before revealing anything sensitive:

  1. Letter of Intent (LOI) — a written, non-binding offer stating price range and terms. It filters browsers from buyers.
  2. Proof of funds (POF) — evidence they can actually close at your price point.

Anyone unwilling to provide these was never going to wire the money.

Step 5: Negotiate on the buyer's real fears

Every objection maps to one fear: "the profit stops when you leave." Answer it directly — show the SOPs, offer a 2–4 week transition period, walk them through supplier continuity, and be honest about ad fatigue and what you'd fix next. Honesty about weaknesses, paired with data, closes better than hype ever does.

Step 6: Close through escrow — always

Non-negotiable rules for the money moment:

  • 100% of funds through a third-party escrow provider. Never direct wires, never crypto "for speed," never partial payments outside escrow.
  • Never hand over logins before escrow is funded. The store transfers when the money is secured — not before.
  • Define the handover checklist in writing: Shopify store transfer, domain, ad accounts, pixel, email list, supplier introductions, socials.

Escrow protects both sides: the buyer knows the asset is real, you know the money is real.

Step 7: Transfer, transition, done

Plan a short transition window (two weeks is standard for dropshipping) covering supplier introductions, ad account walkthroughs, and support handoff. Keep it scoped and time-boxed in the contract — then get paid in full through escrow and move to your next thing.

Common mistakes that kill deals

  • Listing publicly with your store name visible — and getting your winning product cloned mid-sale.
  • Negotiating with buyers who never showed proof of funds.
  • Books that don't reconcile with Shopify data (instant trust collapse).
  • Shutting the store down first. A dead store is a discount; a paused-but-documented store is an asset. More on that: Don't Shut Down Your Shopify Store — Sell It Instead.
